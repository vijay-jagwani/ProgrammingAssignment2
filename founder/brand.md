# Brand brief: supply chain simulation game

Status: working draft. The `founder/` pack (idea, marketing, competitors, panel) is not in this repo, so the positioning below comes from the product brief only: a browser game for running a supply chain month by month (forecasting, production, truckload vs intermodal transport, pricing, trading with rival teams), used for solo practice, classroom and team workshops, and training for ops, planning, procurement and logistics people.

## What this check can and cannot do

- Done: public web searches for ten candidate names against existing logistics, supply chain and game brands.
- Not done: official trademark register searches, domain availability, and social handle availability. Those need a person to run them (links below). Nothing here is legal advice, and nothing is registered until you register it.

**Recommendation: Bufferbay.** It names the central idea of the game (buffer stock) in a word the audience already uses, and the harbour image suggests freight. Its risk is that "buffer" is also an IT term, so the tagline should make the supply chain meaning obvious.

## 3. Promise, tagline and voice

**One-line promise:** Every decision in the game costs or saves real money, so you learn the trade-offs by making them.

**Tagline options**
1. Run the supply chain. Feel every trade-off.
2. Your stock, your trucks, your call, month by month.
3. Learn supply chain by running one.

**Voice in three adjectives**
- **Practical.** Do: state the cost and the effect plainly ("Truckload costs $X more per unit but arrives in one week"). Don't: use jargon without a plain-English gloss.
- **Dry.** Do: let the numbers be funny ("Your warehouse is full of beans nobody wants"). Don't: use exclamation marks or hype.
- **Encouraging.** Do: treat a bad month as a lesson ("Stockouts happened. Here's what caused them"). Don't: shame the player.

**Before and after, from the LinkedIn draft**
- Before: "Supply chain is hard to learn from slides. So I built a game for it."
- After: "Supply chain is hard to learn from slides. Bufferbay lets you run one, month by month."

## 4. The look (brief)

**Colour**

| Role | Hex | Job | Contrast check |
|------|-----|-----|----------------|
| Deep navy | `#1B2A41` | Primary text, headings, logo | 12.61:1 on sand (AAA) |
| Harbour teal | `#0F766E` | Actions, links, highlighted lanes | 5.47:1 white on teal (AA); 4.78:1 teal on sand (AA) |
| Signal amber | `#E0A100` | Alerts and warnings only, never body text | 6.37:1 navy on amber (AA) |
| Warehouse sand | `#F4EFE6` | Page background | Base for the checks above |

**Type**
- Display: Fraunces (SIL Open Font Licence, free from Google Fonts)
- Text: Inter (SIL Open Font Licence, free from Google Fonts)

Both are licensed for commercial use under OFL. Check the licence file before you embed them.

**Logo brief**
- Must say: "Bufferbay" in one glance, and suggest a stock buffer or a harbour.
- Must work: a sign, a cup, a 32px app icon, a black-and-white print.
- Concept direction: a wordmark plus a mark made of three stacked blocks with one block set slightly apart (the buffer), or two nodes joined by a curved lane.
- Avoid: truck clip-art, gears (generic "industry"), anything resembling a shipping company's logo, and any use of General Mills colours or packaging style.
- Generated concepts, if made with an image tool, are concepts only, not a finished logo.

**The first five touchpoints**
1. The homepage hero and game title: "Bufferbay" with the promise underneath.
2. The landing-page role picker: each role described in one plain line.
3. The end-of-game standings: one headline result per team, in the voice above.
4. The first social post: the LinkedIn draft in section 3.
5. The reply to the first complaint: acknowledge the cost, explain the mechanic, and say what changes if it was a bug.
